# Personas and Jobs To Be Done

ScaleScape serves learners from first contact through advanced exploration. The interface is designed from the least-assumed-knowledge path, while experienced users can skip guidance and access the same musical model directly.

## Primary Persona: Curious Newcomer

**Alex** enjoys music but may not play an instrument or know terms such as interval, mode, or tonic. Alex wants to hear what these ideas mean without studying definitions first.

### Job

When I become curious about a musical sound, I want the app to guide me through hearing and manipulating it so I can understand the idea without prior theory knowledge.

### Success Signal

Alex completes a guided comparison, recognizes that one note changed, and can replay or locate that change with the app's help.

## Secondary Persona: Intermediate Guitarist

**Marina** has played guitar for several years. She knows common pentatonic shapes and can follow songs, but modal language feels abstract. She wants to improvise with intention rather than search for safe notes.

### Job

When I am improvising over a minor progression, I want to understand which interval gives a mode its character so I can choose notes deliberately.

### Success Signal

Marina can locate and intentionally play the characteristic degree of a mode over a drone, then describe how it differs from natural minor.

## Secondary Persona: Producer or Keyboard Player

**Lucas** uses a DAW and can build basic chords. He often discovers melodies by trial and error and wants a faster way to understand the color of a mode.

### Job

When I want a specific musical color, I want to hear and compare modes over the same root so I can compose with intent.

### Success Signal

Lucas can compare two modes over the same tonic and choose one based on an audible and theoretical difference.

## Secondary Persona: Multi-Instrument Learner

**Sofia** plays guitar and is learning piano. She understands that scales are abstract structures but has not transferred that understanding between instruments.

### Job

When I learn a scale on one instrument, I want to see the same interval structure on another instrument so the theory does not remain tied to a single fingering pattern.

### Success Signal

Sofia can find the same tonic, third, fifth, and characteristic degree on both instruments.

## Entry Model

The core listening and comparison path requires no prior theory or instrument knowledge. The product MUST:

- Introduce plain-language meaning before technical labels.
- Make instrument interaction understandable through visible feedback and optional guidance.
- Allow learners who already know roots, intervals, or modes to skip guidance.
- Never require a learner to identify a physical instrument position to complete the listening path.

Playing or studying an instrument enriches the experience but is not an entry requirement.

## Jobs Outside the MVP

- A teacher creating and assigning a complete curriculum.
- An advanced musician analyzing complex substitutions.
- A performer connecting hardware for low-latency evaluation.
- A learner needing a complete course in notation, rhythm, or instrumental technique.

## Discovery Questions

The first validation sessions should answer:

1. Can newcomers complete the first comparison without knowing interval terminology?
2. Is audio context more useful than a familiar static diagram?
3. Do users naturally switch between piano and guitar views?
4. Does the interactive reveal create a memorable insight before the technical label appears?
5. Would users return for short ear-training challenges?
